Who funded the tiebreaker's advisors?

New Jersey's redistricting commission is supposed to balance 6 Democrats, 6 Republicans, and one neutral tiebreaker picked by the state Supreme Court. In 2021 that tiebreaker brought in the Princeton Gerrymandering Project as his analytical staff. The Project is led by a Princeton neuroscience professor and reads like an academic group. Its three publicly listed major funders were all significant Democratic donors, with combined FEC contributions in the hundreds of millions. The tiebreaker picked the Democratic map. The state investigated; the academics were cleared of data manipulation. The funding relationship was never directly addressed.

1THE CAST
Tiebreaker
John E. Wallace
Former NJ Supreme Court Justice, appointed by Chief Justice Stuart Rabner. Cast the deciding 13th vote on the congressional map.
Advisory group
Princeton Gerrymandering Project
Academic group led by Princeton neuroscience professor Sam Wang. Brought into the room by Wallace to provide analysis. How they were selected has not been publicly disclosed.
Dem incumbents at risk
Kim · Gottheimer · Malinowski · Sherrill
NJ-3, NJ-5, NJ-7, NJ-11. The four most competitive seats heading into the new map.
GOP challenger
Tom Kean Jr.
Former NJ Senate Minority Leader. Lost NJ-7 to Malinowski by ~5,000 votes in 2020; won the rematch in 2022 after redistricting.
2THE MAP
Map adopted
Dec. 22, 2021
Wallace cast the tiebreaking vote for the Democratic-submitted map. Filed final Jan. 6, 2022.
NJ-7 before
D+9,000 voters
Democratic registered-voter advantage of roughly 9,000.
NJ-7 after
R+17,000 voters
Republican registered-voter advantage of more than 17,000. The "Democratic" map actually moved GOP-leaning territory into NJ-7.
Why it gets called "Dem map"
Trades NJ-7 for stronger Dem holds elsewhere
The map shored up the other three competitive Dem seats (Kim, Gottheimer, Sherrill) by sacrificing NJ-7 — a net-positive trade for Democrats statewide.

4WHAT IT COST TO FIGHT FOR NJ-7

After the map was set, NJ-7 became the most expensive race in the state. Independent expenditures alone — money from outside PACs, not the candidates themselves — totaled $6.8M in 2022 and $9.4M in 2024.

5WHAT HAPPENED NEXT
Dec 22, 2021
Wallace casts tiebreaking vote. Congressional commission adopts the Democratic-submitted map 7–6.
Jan 2022
NJ Globe publishes the original reporting on PGP's donor base. Senate Minority Leader Steve Oroho calls for a state investigation.
Nov 8, 2022
Kean defeats Malinowski in NJ-7 by ~3 points. The "Democratic" map costs the Democrats the seat anyway.
2023
State Commission of Investigation report finds "no merit" to claims that Sam Wang manipulated data for partisan ends. The funding-relationship question is not directly addressed.
Nov 5, 2024
Kean wins reelection by ~5 points after $9.4M in outside spending floods NJ-7, dominated by GOP-aligned Congressional Leadership Fund.

↺ NoteThe "Democratic" map cost Democrats NJ-7 in both 2022 and 2024. The largest outside money in the district over those two cycles came from Republican-aligned PACs. This story is about how influence flows through redistricting — not which party "won" this particular map.

⚖ EDITORIAL TRANSPARENCY · WHAT WE'RE CLAIMING AND WHAT WE AREN'T
✓ Verified from primary sources (FEC filings, court records, state reports) The Princeton Gerrymandering Project lists Simons Foundation, Schmidt Futures, and Educational Ventures as major funders. All three principals are documented FEC donors to Democratic candidates and committees, including the four NJ Democratic incumbents most affected by redistricting. The donation amounts cited above are itemized in FEC filings. Wallace was the tiebreaker. Wallace picked the Democratic map. The SCI report cleared the academics of data manipulation.
⚠ Reported but contested Whether the PGP's funding base influenced the analytical choices it offered Wallace is an inference, not a documented finding. Sam Wang and the PGP have consistently maintained their methodology was apolitical. The SCI investigation did not find data manipulation. Reasonable people disagree on whether the appearance of the funding relationship was a meaningful issue regardless of intent.
◯ Unresolved / still reporting Princeton University declined the NJ Globe's request to publish a full donor list for the PGP. How Wallace specifically came to select PGP as his advisors has not been publicly explained. The full universe of PGP funders therefore remains undisclosed.
