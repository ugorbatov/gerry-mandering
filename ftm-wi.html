Wisconsin · 2023 & 2025 Supreme Court races

Two consecutive record-shattering judicial races, $151M+ in combined spending, and the state-party loophole that made it possible.

STATE LEGISLATIVE MAP $151M+ ACROSS 2 RACES LIBERALS FLIPPED COURT CLARKE V. WEC · 2023 NEW MAPS · FEB 2024

Buy the court that rules on the maps.

Wisconsin doesn't have a redistricting commission and the legislature is locked. So the money goes to the only body that can change the maps anyway: the Wisconsin Supreme Court. In 2023 a single justice's election cost $51 million — the most ever spent on a judicial race in U.S. history. Two years later the rematch cost $100M+, doubling the record. Both races turned on a state-party loophole that lets billionaires write unlimited checks to political parties, which then write unlimited checks to officially-nonpartisan judicial candidates.

1THE CAST
2023 winner
Janet Protasiewicz
Milwaukee County circuit judge. Campaigned openly on calling Wisconsin's maps "rigged." Defeated Daniel Kelly by ~11 points. Flipped the court to a 4–3 liberal majority for the first time since 2008.
2025 winner
Susan Crawford
Dane County circuit judge. Defeated Brad Schimel by ~10 points. Preserved the liberal majority through the next decade of redistricting litigation.
The case
Clarke v. Wisconsin Elections Commission
Filed Aug 2023, decided Dec 22, 2023 — five months after Protasiewicz took her seat. 4–3 ruling struck down legislative maps as unconstitutional under the contiguity clause.
The remedy
New maps · Feb 19, 2024
Legislature passed remedial maps after court order; Gov. Evers signed. Maps now used in 2024+ elections; significantly more competitive than predecessors.
2THE MECHANISM
Direct candidate limit
$20,000 per individual
Wisconsin caps individual donations to Supreme Court candidates at $20,000.
State-party limit
Unlimited
No cap on how much an individual can give a state political party.
Party-to-candidate limit
Unlimited
No cap on how much a state party can transfer to its candidate.
Practical effect
Billionaires → state party → candidate
Donors who hit the $20K direct cap simply route additional millions through the party. The 2023 Wisconsin Democratic Party transferred ~$9M to Protasiewicz alone — most of her campaign's funding.

6WHAT HAPPENED NEXT
2011
GOP-controlled legislature enacts maps that survive federal challenges, including the U.S. Supreme Court's 2019 Rucho v. Common Cause decision closing the federal courts to partisan-gerrymandering claims.
Apr 4, 2023
Protasiewicz wins by ~11 points. Flips court to 4–3 liberal majority. Race cost $51M — most expensive judicial election in U.S. history at that point.
Aug 2, 2023
Clarke v. WEC filed. 19 plaintiffs challenge legislative maps as unconstitutional. Republican legislators move to impeach Protasiewicz before she can hear the case; effort fails.
Dec 22, 2023
Court strikes down the maps 4–3 (Karofsky majority, joined by Bradley, Dallet, Protasiewicz). Ruling based on the state constitution's contiguity clause.
Feb 19, 2024
Gov. Evers signs new maps passed by the legislature after court remedial process. New maps used in 2024+ elections.
Apr 1, 2025
Crawford defeats Schimel by ~10 points. Preserves liberal court majority. Race cost $100M+ — new world record, with Elon Musk alone responsible for ~$22M.

↺ NoteBoth 2023 and 2025 winners were Democratic-aligned. The "buy the court" mechanism works for whichever side raises more — it's about the loophole, not the party. The 2025 race had bigger Republican-aligned individual donors (Musk, Uihlein) but Democratic-aligned coordinated party spending still outpaced them.

⚖ EDITORIAL TRANSPARENCY · WHAT WE'RE CLAIMING AND WHAT WE AREN'T
✓ Verified from primary sources (state campaign finance filings, court records, Brennan Center tallies) Total spending figures ($51M in 2023, $100M+ in 2025) come from the Brennan Center for Justice and the Wisconsin Democracy Campaign. Individual donor amounts, party-to-candidate transfers, and the $20,000 individual cap with unlimited party transfers are documented in Wisconsin Ethics Commission filings. The Clarke v. WEC ruling and new-map signing dates are court and legislative records.
⚠ Reported but contested Protasiewicz pledged to recuse from cases brought directly by the Wisconsin Democratic Party (her main funder). Critics argue she should have recused from Clarke because the plaintiffs were represented by attorneys whose firms had received party-linked funding; she declined. Whether judges who openly campaign on substantive policy positions (as Protasiewicz did on "rigged maps") undermine judicial independence is a normative debate, not a documented finding.
◯ Unresolved / still reporting The full universe of donors to outside-spending groups remains incomplete — Wisconsin permits 501(c)(4) "dark money" groups to fund independent expenditures without disclosing donors. Musk's $22M+ figure is the Brennan Center's tally based on what's been disclosed; the true figure may be higher. Several outside groups on both sides have not filed final reports.
