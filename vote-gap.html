HOW MAPS DISTORT REPRESENTATION
PK
Packing
Concentrate the opposing party's voters into as few districts as possible, where they win by huge margins. A packed district here is any seat won with 75% or more — every extra vote is "wasted" since the seat is already locked.
CR
Cracking
Split the opposing party's voters across many districts so they never reach a majority. A cracked district is one where the losing party gets 35–49% — close, but the map ensures they never quite win.
EG
Efficiency Gap
The legal-academic metric. Counts every wasted vote — votes for losers, plus winners' votes beyond 50%+1 — and shows which side wasted more. Above ±7% is the gerrymandering red line.
WV
Wasted votes
A vote is "wasted" when it doesn't help elect anyone. Every vote for the losing candidate is wasted. So is every vote for the winner beyond what they needed to win. Maps with low wasted votes are efficient. Maps where one side wastes far more are gerrymandered.
DP
Disproportionality
The simplest measure: how far seat share strays from vote share. A party that gets 51% of votes but 80% of seats has 29 points of disproportionality. This alone doesn't prove gerrymandering — single-member districts naturally amplify majorities — but extreme gaps are a starting flag.
SD
Single-district states
Seven states have only one House seat: AK, DE, MT, ND, SD, VT, WY. They cannot be gerrymandered — the "map" is the state line. Their Senate seats are also statewide, immune to redistricting. They appear here for completeness but never in gerrymandering rankings.
WHY THE SENATE IS DIFFERENT
⚖
Senate seats are statewide — every Senator represents the whole state, not a drawn district. So there's no gerrymandering of the Senate. But the Senate's structural disproportionality — every state gets 2 Senators regardless of population — is enormous and beyond the scope of redistricting reform. California's 39 million people get the same Senate representation as Wyoming's 580,000.
